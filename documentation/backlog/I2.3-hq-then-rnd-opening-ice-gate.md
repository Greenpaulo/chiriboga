# I2.3 Gate HQ-first, R&D-second opening ICE against current I2

**Roadmap item:** I2.3 · **Depends on:** I2, F4 · **Sets:** playable sets (`documentation/card-sets.md`)
**Read first:** [I2.2 investigation](code-review/I2.2-rnd-protection-lateness-investigation.md), [paired findings](../corp-ai/measurements/I2.2-rnd-protection-lateness.md), `documentation/ai-principles.md`, `documentation/corp-ai/principles.md`, [install planning](../corp-ai/architecture.md#install-planning-today), [gate workflow](../ai-planning.md#acceptance-gates)
**Verified against code:** 18bb6e6 (2026-10-22)

## Goal

Run the owner's requested comparison, specified 2026-10-22:

- **Option A:** the first ICE installed goes on **HQ**, the second goes on
  **R&D**, and the third and subsequent ICE use current I2 placement.
- **Option B:** current adopted I2 throughout, without the opening constraint.

The owner explicitly chose HQ first, then R&D; this is not an I2-selected
order. Decide whether this opening rule improves central defence without
undoing I2's scoring, economy, win-rate or deception gains. The investigation's
retain disposition establishes B as the control; it does not settle this
untested alternative. Complete the comparison before freezing I3's design.

## Current behaviour

- `iceMarginalSecurity` defaults to true. `_marginalIceInstallOptions()` ranks
  admitted ICE/server pairs by affordability group, marginal score, then walk
  order; it has no HQ-first/R&D-second requirement. `_rankedInstallOptionsCore()`
  consumes that list and also offers a new-server ICE fallback.
- `_iceInstallOptions()` preserves card suitability and affordability lists.
  A card can be in the low-priority list despite sufficient printed rez funds
  because `AIWorthwhileIce` rejects its present suitability (for example Magnet).
- `_recordProtectionInstall()` records a preferred protection action through
  `_returnPreference()`, before completed installation. It is not an oracle
  for the first two completed ICE installs and does not cover every install.
- I2.2 reproduced later R&D protection in 700 paired diagnostic games:
  conditional first R&D ICE turn 1.280 → 2.026, never protected 4 → 40.
  Its 7,000-pair adoption evidence shows R&D theft rising while HQ theft,
  total theft and Corp wins improve. Opening allocation has both adverse and
  favourable replay examples; those observations do not prove A is better.
- Existing `iceInstallsByServer` measures completed installs by destination
  and first turn, with zero meaning none. `centralStolen.points` is exactly
  HQ plus R&D theft; it excludes Archives. The adoption checks below use
  existing metrics; the new diagnostic collector is a required setup task.

**Mulligan history, checked 2026-10-22:** commit `7591eb4` (2026-09-14)
strengthened opening-hand checks; its parent already contained a fewer-than-two
affordable-ICE rejection, and the change added overlapping checks. Commit
`41a10b0` (2026-09-23, finding 04) replaced these with the current hand-score
versus expected-redraw-score comparison. It deliberately permits keeping a
one-ICE/strong-economy hand. Neither that commit nor finding 04 records a
win-rate-based rejection of the two-ICE rule. The historical regression report
shows a 36.0% → 37.0% checkpoint change at that commit, not an established
win-rate loss; this is not a clean current-policy gate or causal proof.
[F5](../corp-ai/specs/F5-mulligan-weight-calibration.md) remains proposed.

## Design

**Classification:** strategic preference between legal choices, requiring a
default-off Corp option `openingHqThenRdIce` and an F4 adoption gate.
`iceMarginalSecurity=true` remains explicit in **both** arms. No calibration
changes, central score bonuses, other I2 changes or new card-specific rules.

1. Keep a per-game ordinal of **completed Corp ICE installations**. Before
   install 1, restrict voluntary ICE destinations to HQ; before install 2,
   restrict them to R&D. Starting from install 3, return to B's destination
   policy. Rank eligible cards for the required destination using unchanged
   I2 affordability groups and marginal scores. This is a hard destination
   constraint, not a bonus that a sufficiently attractive remote can beat.
2. Apply it consistently to the main protection list, fallback ICE list,
   install selection, and other AI-controlled ICE destination choices,
   including card-granted installs. Prevent a later fallback or direct route
   from silently installing the opening ICE on a remote or doubling HQ.
   Normal non-ICE choices, drawing, economy and action ordering stay intact;
   this ticket does not force ICE to be the first two **actions** or add draw
   pressure to find a second ICE. A one-ICE hand may still leave R&D bare.
3. Keep engine legality, card-owned suitability and existing affordability
   groups. If no ICE can be offered at the required destination, do not
   substitute another voluntary ICE destination; take an otherwise available
   non-ICE action or decline an optional install. A high remote score, low
   central stake, poor economy or bluff posture is not an escape from A.
4. Shared principle 4 still governs forced actions and immediate game-winning
   or game-losing tactics. A rules-forced install with no legal required-central
   destination, or an immediate outcome override, must be identified and
   recorded as an explicit exception, never hidden as normal compliance.
   The literal completed-install ordinal still advances for such an install;
   do not turn this experiment into a renewable central-coverage quota.
   Report exception counts and replay each distinct reason. Do not widen
   this exception to ordinary strategic urgency.
5. Advance the ordinal once, at actual installation completion, across all
   routes. Ranking, hypotheticals, selecting a preference, cancelled installs,
   moving installed ICE and rez events do not advance it. Trashing/bouncing
   an installed ICE does not undo a completed install; reinstalling that card
   is another completed install. Reset only for a new game. Determine the
   completion integration point and every affected caller during implementation
   validation; `_recordProtectionInstall()` alone is insufficient.
6. Log ordinal, required/actual destination, candidate group/score and the
   rule or exception that decided it. Repeated planning and observation must
   preserve live state and RNG. Option-off choices and logs retain their
   existing behaviour; existing green expectations are unchanged.

7. Add passive `openingCentralCoverage` measurements. At actual Corp turn-end
   boundaries 1 and 2, snapshot the **current** HQ/R&D ICE counts, not just
   whether an installation happened earlier. Extend the harness's Corp
   `turnEnd` observation payload with those counts; do not add a decision
   consumer. A collector reports `turn1.observed`, `turn1.both`,
   `turn2.observed`, `turn2.both`, where `both` is 1 only when at least one
   ICE is currently installed on **each** central at that boundary. Missing
   boundaries are unobserved, not successes or ordinary failures. Emit no
   extra AI evaluations, card hooks or random calls. These are planned
   measurements; neither the payload extension nor collector exists yet.
8. Hold the current mulligan policy and main-phase draw/economy ordering
   identical in A and B. Record the initially offered hand's ICE count,
   keep/mulligan choice, post-mulligan hand's ICE count and printed rez-cost
   distribution, then the ICE available after the compulsory first draw.
   Counts use Corp-known information; affordability/suitability must be
   distinguished from printed card count and from the funds needed to rez
   two ICE together. Add these as passive `openingCentralCoverage` samples.
   Opening mulligan strata are defined before A/B diverge, so record matched
   cohorts of 0, 1 and 2+ opening ICE and paired boundary outcomes within each.
   Do not retune mulligans to make A work during its placement gate.

This ticket is ready to plan, not an approved implementation plan. Follow
the implement-ticket plan gate before changing shared install selection or
an engine completion hook. Re-ground the chosen completion mechanism first.

## Safety and information boundary

Only Corp-known cards and public board/history may affect the policy. Never
inspect R&D order, hidden Runner cards or Runner AI state. Add no card-title
checks. Record policy progress from real completed actions, not speculative
evaluations. Baseline observation must not create candidate state or consume
randomness. No AI option is enabled by default before its gate passes.

## Test scenarios

1. Opening with enough usable ICE: A's first completed ICE goes on HQ, second
   on R&D, third follows unchanged I2 ranking, even when a remote outranks the
   centrals. Assert reasons as well as actual engine installs.
2. One ICE and a valuable scoring remote (include btl-kit/1610): A sends that
   ICE to HQ; B retains the recorded I2 allocation. Do not pretend A already
   protects R&D without a second ICE.
3. Second ICE when HQ's marginal score still wins: A sends it to R&D.
   No fallback, direct card install or affordability-group transition bypasses it.
4. No usable offered ICE, optional install with no permitted destination,
   unaffordable-but-offered ICE, and card suitability rejection. Preserve each
   contract; no illegal install, invented affordability or remote fallback.
5. Repeated rankings, hypothetical installs and cancellation do not advance
   the ordinal. Actual installs across ordinary and card-granted routes do;
   rez, movement, trash and bounce do not falsely count or reset it.
6. Forced legality and immediate-outcome exceptions are exact, logged and
   deterministic; ordinary remote urgency is not such an exception.
7. A hidden-information substitution leaves choices unchanged. With A off,
   existing I2 decision fixtures/snapshots pass unchanged. With observation
   on/off, actual choices, randomness and completed-install histories match.
8. Use the I2.2 favourable remote/HQ examples (btl-kit/1603, /1619, /1663,
   /484), adverse /1610 and never-protected /1622 as qualitative replay
   contrasts. These already-seen seeds are development evidence, not adoption
   games. Include early-R&D examples pd-tao/1601 and neh-zahya/1601.
9. Scripted boundary observation: HQ-only, R&D-only, both, neither; ICE
   installed then removed/moved before turn end; unrezzed ICE; no install;
   game ending before turn 1/2 completes; failed games. Boundary occupancy
   must match engine state, with observation-on/off decision/RNG identity.
10. Opening-hand samples distinguish initially offered and redraw hands,
    zero/one/two-or-more ICE, expensive or unsuitable ICE, and the mandatory
    first draw. A and B must have matching mulligan decisions/hands for the
    same paired seed; any mismatch is an unexpected second treatment.

## Acceptance gate

F4 gate. Option `openingHqThenRdIce` (Corp AI), off in the baseline **B**
and on in the candidate **A**, with `iceMarginalSecurity=true` in both.
Committed `core-v1` deck pool, all seven pairs, paired seeds **5001–6000**,
1,000 games per deck pair (7,000 per arm), bootstrap 95% intervals. The larger
sample is fixed before candidate results because the pooled deception guards
were noisy at smaller samples in I2. These seeds are disjoint from I2/I2.2's
recorded calibration, diagnostic and adoption cohorts. If another experiment
has already exposed this range, freeze a replacement before any A results.
Collectors: `centralStolen`, `iceInstallsByServer`, `corpInsolventTurns`,
`strandedUnrezzedIceCost`, `bluffSingleVariableCorrelation`, `realizedIceByRootType`,
`openingCentralCoverage` (adds boundary observed/both indicators and opening-hand samples, defined above; diagnostic only).
Starts: none — this is an opening policy, measured from real deck starts.

Before the full gate, run the same command with `--quick` and replace
`--seeds 5001-6000` with the already-exposed development range
`--seeds 401-450`; explicit seeds take precedence over the CLI's usual quick
sample size. Record at least one changed game. Keep adoption seeds unexamined
until the full frozen run. This is reachability/setup evidence only.
Verify the reports' effective options: only `openingHqThenRdIce` may differ,
and I2 must be on in both. No gate result exists yet. Do not relax guards, switch primary
metrics, select winning decks or add seeds after seeing A's results.

**Required diagnostics, not substitute adoption criteria:** show A versus B's
share of games with at least one installed ICE on **both** HQ and R&D at
the end of Corp **turn 1** and **turn 2**. For each boundary report
`sum(both) / sum(observed)`, the observed numerator/denominator, and the total
games in the arm, plus games ending before the boundary and all failures.
Bootstrap the paired difference on games observed at that boundary in both
arms, reporting the retained/excluded pair counts. Also show the all-game
confirmed-coverage lower bound over the full successful-game cohort,
`sum(both for successful games) / successful games`, excluding failed games
from both numerator and denominator, labelled as
a lower bound because unobserved boundaries are unknown. Break out opening
ICE strata and deck pair. ICE present can be unrezzed and is not a guarantee
of stopping a breach. First-install turn or “ever covered both” cannot
substitute for this occupancy measurement.

For HQ and R&D
separately report first-ICE timing conditional on installation, never-protected
counts, the fraction bare through turn 2 or earlier game end, early-end
censoring, theft and successful/failed game counts; include per-deck variation
and paired uncertainty for defined all-game indicators. Never treat a zero
first-turn metric as an early install or drop no-install games silently.
Report the first two completed destinations and all tactical/forced exceptions.
Do not pool away decks where A loses beneficial remote preparation. Passing
timing diagnostics alone cannot establish that A plays better.
