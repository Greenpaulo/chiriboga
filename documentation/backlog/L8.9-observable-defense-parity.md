## Goal

Explain which observed installation routes and later lifecycle events produce
the agenda/trap defense difference, and give installation work reusable passive
measurement. A difference is not itself a bug, and zero ICE is not itself a bad
install. This ticket does not promise parity, a win-rate improvement, or a new
installation rule.

## Current behaviour

`_rankedInstallOptionsCore()` has different admission routes for ordinary HVTs,
card-hook-selected installs and the existing Snare! “Specific case”. The latter
prefers the strongest protected empty remote and falls back to a new bare remote.
The observer records results from those routes without changing them. The shared
collector's headline populations remain unrevealed agenda/trap roots; ordinary
assets/upgrades are combined as `other` in lifecycle evidence, not a complete
model of remote-server topology or deck strategy.

### Coverage and interpretation limits

A shell or asset-spam deck may intentionally create many cheap unprotected
remotes, including ordinary assets alongside traps. The evidence here did not
establish representative shell-plan coverage or the desirability of protecting
every root. No “must have ICE” rule or mechanical count equalization follows.
I3/I5 must evaluate that counterexample and state any coverage gap. I3.1 records
deck composition/mechanic evidence; it must not infer shell play from ICE density
alone, a deck name or the number of remotes currently on the board.

Saved lifecycle `other` records do not distinguish ordinary assets from upgrades
or retain enough card identity for an asset-spam reconstruction. Headline rows
cover agendas/traps; there is no full empty-remote census, complete HQ hold-age
history, or denominator for opportunities ending without installation. Aggregate
`installOutcomes` metrics cannot reconstruct those missing histories. A later
consumer needs scoped new observation and identity tests if its claim needs them.

Holding can change timing/frequency, HQ exposure, discard and installed-class
mixture, so a smaller installed ICE-count tell need not mean less information
for the Runner. Holding is not a queue or a promise to install later. Snare!'s
existing HQ access effect remains live if affordable; Archives does not have
that effect. These findings are preserved for I5, not an adopted hold policy.

Power planning is also retained: detecting a two-percentage-point win-rate drop
with 80% power/two-sided 5% needs about 1,960 matched pairs at 10% winner
discordance or 3,923 at 20%. Controls cannot estimate a future policy's discordance;
2,800 pairs are not universally sufficient. Those planning approximations do not
replace the gate's actual confidence-interval regression rule.

## Safety and information boundary

Observation must leave choices, candidate ordering, RNG, caches, gameplay logs
and hidden-Runner information use unchanged. Record unknown/uncomputed values;
do not call evaluators just to fill diagnostic gaps. Class labels are harness
truth, never policy inputs. Preserve failed records and invalidate failed or
incomplete measurements rather than turning them into outcomes or zeros.

## Test scenarios

1. Install-time ICE count differs correctly from later Runner-turn exposure;
   requested and committed placement remain distinct.
2. Actual helper returns, card-hook/direct routes and offered alternatives are
   captured without re-evaluation/re-sorting; missing/Infinity inputs stay explicit.
3. ICE additions/removals, reinstall/overwrite, shared roots, posture transitions
   and score/steal/trash/game-end endpoints are reconciled without double counts.
4. Hidden Runner substitutions and throwing observers preserve gameplay identity.
5. Whole-game analysis includes zero-install games and rejects sparse/invalid
   inference; complete-case mode validates every scheduled key and failure record.
