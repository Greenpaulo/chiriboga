**Roadmap item:** I3.1 · **Depends on:** I10, F4 · **Sets:** playable sets (`documentation/card-sets.md`)

**Verified against code:** fe9f1d3 (2026-10-13)

## Goal

Summarise the Corp's own starting deck as explainable game-plan signals, then
check whether those signals distinguish the pool's plans. This item builds a
read-only profile and collector; no decision consumes it. A later consumer
needs a concrete decision gap, a bounded adjustment and its own strategic gate.
I3 is a sequencing dependency to prioritise the core install planner, not a
runtime requirement for reading a decklist.

## Current behaviour

- There is no `gamePlanProfile` or `AIPlanRole` implementation. I3's proposed
  remote roles describe current servers and candidates, not starting deck plans.
- `_potentialAdvancement()` and fast-advance tutor preferences already reason
  about advancement support. Deck profiling does not replace those tactical
  checks or establish that a particular scoring line is affordable.
- `_expectedOpeningHandScore()` uses card composition, and mulligan evaluation
  forms a redraw pool from HQ and R&D. Own-deck reasoning is already allowed;
  the missing piece is a persistent, explainable summary for later planning.
- The committed `core-v1` pool contains `leo-topan` (LEO Glacier) and
  `neh-zahya` (a precon with Biotic Labor and SanSan support), alongside hybrid
  plans. Their compositions are useful contrast cases, not proof that a new
  classifier or consumer works.

## Design

### Profile contract

Behind default-off option `gamePlanProfile`, capture the starting Corp identity
and the multiset of deck cards before opening-hand decisions. For resumed start
boards, require a recorded starting decklist; report `unavailable` if provenance
is absent rather than treating the remaining R&D as the original deck.

Start with transparent composition signals, not a fitted archetype classifier:

- `iceDensity = iceCopies / deckCopies`;
- `assetDensity = assetCopies / deckCopies`, with separate asset/upgrade counts
  and printed rez/trash-cost distributions (retain unknown printed data);
- declared access-punishment/Ambush and asset-economy mechanic evidence, with
  contribution/coverage records; a declaration is not an estimated chance of
  access, payoff or a reason to install without ICE;
- `advancementSupportDensity = declaredSupportCopies / deckCopies`, where a
  card declaring `AIFastAdvance` or `AIFastAdvanceCounters` counts once;
- agenda counts by printed advancement requirement and points, and ICE counts
  by printed rez cost and strength;
- identity and scoring-upgrade mechanic evidence, separately attributed;
- `coverage`: known, unsupported and unknown feature contributions, with reasons.

The densities lie in [0, 1] and are independent. ICE and advancement densities
are initial proxies for glacier investment and advancement support; asset density
and cost/mechanic distributions describe composition. None is a probability,
a complete plan classifier or a recommended policy weight. Do not call advancement support proof of same-turn scoring:
conditional advancement, never-advance support and click creation differ.

Reuse existing card properties and hooks first. Do not invoke stateful tactical
hooks on an imaginary board merely to classify a deck. When a material mechanic
is absent from existing declarations, audit examples across the playable sets
and add a narrow, declarative `AIPlanFeatures()` only for that gap. It reports
mechanic facts and prerequisites, not global strategy scores. Freeze and document
its schema before adding declarations; missing declarations remain unknown.
Identity contributions and synergies stay as evidence records, not uncalibrated
bonuses to either density. Count a support card once even if several hooks
identify it. No card-title lists in the profile implementation.

Store a canonical, order-independent result on the AI instance. Collector
`gamePlanProfile` emits each deck's feature counts, densities, source coverage,
identity evidence and contributing card IDs once per game. Diagnostic output
uses the collector channel, not gameplay logs. No RNG is used.

### Shell and asset-spam evidence (owner scope note, 2026-10-19)

The owner identified shell/asset-spam as a counterexample to treating bare remotes
as installation failures: a plan can deploy many cheap unprotected assets/traps
and make repeated checking or trashing costly, while protecting selected servers.
This item should test whether starting-deck evidence distinguishes that plan
from glacier and advancement-support plans, including hybrids. It must not label
a deck “shell” from a name, low ICE density alone, or the live count of remotes.
Freeze any additional declarative feature schema only after auditing real scoped
examples; do not call tactical hooks on hypothetical boards to fill it.

[L8.9](../../backlog/code-review/L8.9-observable-defense-parity.md) delivers passive installation
and lifecycle tools, not a must-have-ICE policy. Its current seven-pair evidence
has no established representative shell-plan stratum. Saved ordinary assets and
upgrades are combined as `other`; those reports cannot recover a full asset-spam
plan, empty-remote census or HQ hold history. Use its
[tool recipe](../../ai-batch-harness.md#using-install-observations-in-the-i-tickets)
for per-deck/route observations where available, and identify what additional
non-policy telemetry is needed to compare a profile with actual deployment.
Do not reconstruct missing features or silently change the frozen pool.

Before implementation, choose and document at least one supported shell/asset-spam
example, a glacier contrast and a hybrid, with known decklist provenance and
mechanic evidence. If the committed pool does not support that comparison,
record the coverage gap and propose a fixed diagnostic deck/start extension
through the normal workflow; no extra games are authorized by this note.
Composition alone does not establish how the deck should play or that a policy
helps. I3 retains role/occupancy decisions, I4 agenda install/hold, and I5
asset/trap install/hold. They can cover shell-like board counterexamples without
waiting for I3.1; adopting a profile input later needs its consumer's own gate.

### Evidence for a later consumer

Report the pool's distributions and counterexamples: ICE-heavy fast advance,
conditional advancement tools, hybrids, and plans dominated by identity effects.
A hybrid can have strong evidence; do not reduce confidence merely because it
has two plans. Coverage measures missing evidence, not archetype purity.

Before proposing a consumer, identify a real I3/I4/I5 decision where a starting
bias could help, distinguish it from existing tactical planning, and state how
public game state overrides it. Do not automatically add I3.2 or make I4/I5
wait for this item. Profile computation alone establishes no playing benefit.

## Safety and information boundary

Own starting deck multiset and identity only; never use R&D order or hidden
Runner identities. The profile is deterministic and read-only. No profile field
enters `_protectionScore()`, deterministic security, reservations, postures,
install rankings or scoring admission in this item.

## Test scenarios

1. Exact deck counts and densities reconcile with the per-card contributions;
   a card with two support declarations counts once.
2. Deck permutation and redistribution between opening HQ and R&D leave the
   captured starting profile identical; no R&D position affects it.
3. Hidden Runner Grip/Stack substitutions change nothing.
4. ICE-heavy advancement support produces both signals, without an archetype
   switch or an automatic low-confidence label.
5. Missing/conditional mechanic evidence remains explicitly unsupported or
   unknown; no tactical line is inferred from the support count alone.
6. Identity evidence is separate from card-copy counts. A throwing optional
   feature hook leaves game state unchanged and records unsupported evidence.
7. Start boards without a starting decklist report unavailable; known starts
   use the recorded original multiset, not only remaining cards.
8. A supported shell/asset-spam example differs on recorded composition/mechanic
   evidence from a glacier contrast; a hybrid retains both signals. Low ICE
   density alone and shuffled deck names never supply an archetype label.
9. Asset/upgrade counts and cost distributions reconcile with the starting
   multiset; missing economic/access mechanics stay unknown, not zero payoff.
10. Option off computes nothing. Option on changes no decisions, gameplay logs,
   policy RNG state, postures or evaluation caches; output is collector-only.

## Acceptance gate

N/A — deterministic fix (principle 4): observation-only identity invariant.
With `gamePlanProfile` off/on, run the committed F4 pool on matching revisions
and 200 paired seeds per deck pair. Every successful pair must match `winner`,
`turns`, `logHash` and `decisionHash`, with no new failures. Assert policy RNG
identity in deterministic tests as well as checking whole-game outcomes.
Record commands, pool/configuration hashes, counts, runtime and profile coverage.
No empirical archetype-separation threshold is an adoption gate because there
is no policy adoption. Weak separation is a reason not to propose a consumer.
