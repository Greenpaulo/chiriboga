# I7.2 Root install, operation and advance versus other actions

**Roadmap item:** I7.2 · **Depends on:** I4, I5, I6, F4 · **Sets:** playable sets (`documentation/card-sets.md`)
**Read first:** `documentation/ai-principles.md`, `documentation/corp-ai/principles.md`, `documentation/corp-ai/specs/install-decisions-design.md`
**Verified against code:** 376f32c (2026-09-25)

## Goal
Compare the best root install (agenda, asset, upgrade) with playing an
operation, advancing, rezzing, triggering an ability, purging, trashing a
resource or waiting, on the same scale I7.1 uses for ICE, so every main-phase
choice records why it beat the best alternative. (Split from the former I7;
ICE versus credit and draw is I7.1.)

## Current behaviour
`Phase_Main` is an ordered cascade: rez via `Phase_PostAction()`, priority
triggers, kill combos, the almost-done agenda or hostile asset search,
`_criticalBreachDefenseAction()`, opportunity plays, tag punishment,
`_ordinaryPurgeOutcome()`, advancement (when an agenda is almost done, the
economy is sufficient or `_installedAgendaCanBeCompleted()`), the priority
install path, emergency recovery, economy, hand-pressure installs, and finally
`_bestMainPhaseEconomyOption()`. Each step either returns or falls through; no
two steps are compared.

Two known gaps: the critical-central interrupt runs before any install, and its
win exemption covers only an installed almost-done agenda, so a winning agenda
still in HQ that could be installed and fast-advanced this turn is not exempt
(design note edge case 3; bug ticket
[winning-hq-agenda-preempted-by-critical-breach-interrupt.md](../../bugs/winning-hq-agenda-preempted-by-critical-breach-interrupt.md));
and hand pressure can force a low-value install
when a discard or economy line is better.
See [architecture.md: install planning today](../architecture.md#install-planning-today).

## Design
- Use the design note's bands, as I7.1 does: band 3 (forced, winning this
  turn, including the fast-advance-to-win install) is consulted before band 2
  (the critical-central interrupt and other game-losing-breach prevention);
  everything else is band 1 on the credit-equivalent scale.
- Band-1 values: root installs from I4 (`scoringValue`), I5 (`economyValue`,
  `trapValue`) and I6 (`securityGain`); advancing from the same completion
  plan I4 records; operations from their declared hooks (`AIPlayWhenCan`,
  economy and damage hooks); rez and trigger from their existing hooks; each
  with its click and credit `opportunityCost`.
- Purge keeps its conservative outcome contract: `_ordinaryPurgeOutcome()`
  returns a reason (open an immediate score, or secure a staked server) and
  that reason, valued like the outcome it names, is the purge candidate. Do
  not approximate it with an install bonus. Tag-trash is compared the same way.
- Hand-size pressure adds the discarded card's value to the opportunity cost
  of not playing it; it never lifts a candidate above band 2.
- Leave the "hold" slot I7.1 defines for the R1 items.

## Safety and information boundary
Forced and game-winning actions remain hard priorities. Hand-size pressure
affects opportunity cost but never erases tactical safety. The
critical-protection fallback's agenda-flood, economy, last-click and
imperfect-information guards must be preserved.

## Test scenarios
1. A playable economy operation outranks a negative-return economy asset.
2. A guaranteed score outranks speculative protection or economy.
3. Hand-size pressure affects opportunity cost but does not erase tactical
   safety.
4. With a winning agenda in HQ that can be installed and fast-advanced this
   turn and a central above the critical-risk threshold, the Corp installs the
   agenda (today `_criticalBreachDefenseAction()` pre-empts it).
5. An ordinary purge is chosen only for a reason `_ordinaryPurgeOutcome()`
   returns, and loses to a candidate worth more than that outcome.
6. Under hand pressure, discarding a low-value card beats installing a
   negative-value asset into an unprotected server.

## Acceptance gate
Improvement gate: candidate `this.options.actionComparison` on against I0's
baseline (with I2 to I7.1 options on in both arms), with the standard guards
(design note) and:

- Improvement: `pointsScored` per game rises (candidate minus baseline lower
  bound above 0).
- Guards: `pointsStolen` standard guard; `stallTurns` upper bound at most +0.5
  turns per game; `corpInsolventTurns` standard guard.

## Things to consider
- The critical-protection fallback is a bounded safety fallback, not a
  replacement for this comparison; compare it against tutors and operations
  while preserving its guards.
