# L8.10 Trap mechanics and play-value diagnostics

**Roadmap item:** L8.10 · **Depends on:** L8.7, I1, F2, F4 · **Sets:** playable sets (`documentation/card-sets.md`)

## Goal

Provide read-only trap-mechanics diagnostics and measure opportunities to play
traps, without changing decisions. I5 owns any later bounded `trapValue`
conversion and adoption; this item supplies facts and uncertainty instead of
an uncalibrated expected-value score. Consolidates the two prospective L8.10
drafts. L8.8's shared concealment model is independent and is not a prerequisite.

## Current behaviour

- Trap play is not wholly absent: HVT install handling offers Ambushes to
  scoring servers, and a legacy Snare-specific branch offers an affordable
  install. The missing feature is a general, justified payoff breakdown.
- `_accessPunishmentSeverity()` reads installed hidden remote roots through
  `AIPunishesAccess(server)`. The hook reports current planning severity, not
  expected damage, access probability, or a credit-equivalent value. Current
  Urtica/Snare declarations require the card to be in the supplied server;
  simply calling them on an HQ card returns zero rather than its install potential.
- `_calculateBaitFrequency()` converts severity into posture frequency.
  Existing `AIReserveCredits` and `_reserveCreditsForCard()` already declare
  trigger reserves, including Snare's access funding. Do not introduce a
  second reservation system or claim no trigger-funding support exists.
- `_tagPunishmentDeterrence()` checks affordable `AITagPunishment` cards in HQ
  against current public tags; other tutor/damage preferences also exist.
  These are not general trap-plus-operation plans.
- I5's proposed `trapValue` has range 0 to 4 and owns punishment conversion;
  I3 owns roles/displacement and L8.8 proposes shared concealment value.
  None establishes a calibrated access-response model today.
- `core-v1` loads the three playable sets, excluding Vantage Point. L8.7's
  pending scoped hook audit remains necessary; declarations in an in-progress
  set do not establish playable-set coverage.

## Design

### Declarative mechanics

Audit the playable access-punishing cards after L8.7. Design a narrow
`AITrapMechanics(context)` hook from at least Urtica Cipher and Snare!, plus
any different scoped mechanics discovered by the audit. Proposed context names
location (HQ/remote/central), prospective installation, advancement counters
and the Corp's available trigger budget. Freeze the final schema before adding
hooks; it must represent at least:

- trigger location/conditions and enabled/disabled/unknown status;
- damage type/amount, tags and other punishment as separate effects;
- trigger cost and prevention/avoidance qualifications;
- required setup, such as advancement, and unsupported mechanics.

Declare mechanics safely outside a run. Do not change `AIPunishesAccess`'s
contract, invoke gameplay abilities, read hidden Runner cards, or use title
checks in the planner. Reuse `AIReserveCredits` for reservation requirements.
A missing hook yields unknown mechanics, not zero payoff.

### Read-only helper

Behind default-off diagnostic option `trapPlayDiagnostics`, add
`_trapPlayDiagnostics(card, server, context)` with a named breakdown:

- actual installed severity, or `notApplicable` for an HQ card;
- declared effects, setup and trigger affordability under projected costs;
- `damageLine`: `lethalIfAccessed`, `notLethalUnderAssumptions` or `unknown`,
  with public Grip size, damage amount and prevention assumptions recorded;
- actual paid costs separately from reserved liquidity and role displacement;
- access estimate and evidence status, initially `unknown` outside measured
  cohorts; no credit-equivalent scalar and no certain win claim.

Flatline requires damage strictly greater than Grip size. Equal damage empties
Grip without flatlining. A low current Grip is only a snapshot: the Runner can
draw, prevent damage or decline access before the trap fires. Hidden prevention
remains uncertainty; do not present lack of known prevention as guaranteed death.
Account for advancement clicks/credits and trigger funding in the diagnostic
context rather than reporting an unadvanced install's future damage as immediate.
Use an overlay or F2 guard when necessary; probes never enter real-board caches.

### Passive opportunity and access collector

Collector `trapPlayDiagnostics` records one opportunity per real install decision,
with candidate/commitment IDs: eligible traps in HQ, legal alternatives, chosen
action, prospective setup/funding, public Grip size and declared mechanics.
Track actual installs, next Runner-turn public server shape, access, trigger,
damage/prevention and direct trap flatlines. Include origin metadata for starts.
Keep Corp-known security assessments separate from public ICE count, revealed
ICE and public Runner capabilities. Central accesses are a separate cohort;
retaining Snare in HQ can be valuable and is not automatically a missed install.

Define the initial access horizon as the first Runner turn after installation.
An installed trap not accessed before the end of that turn is a non-access;
roots scored/trashed/overwritten earlier and games ending earlier are competing
outcomes, reported separately. Longer-lived traps get separate age cohorts,
not repeated independent trials. Estimate access rates from control-only data
with numerator, denominator, game coverage and intervals clustered within games.
Report sparse strata as insufficient evidence; freeze any training table and
use disjoint seeds for validation. Never calibrate an access probability from
severity or assume the control policy's table transports to a changed policy.

Direct flatlines are attributable outcomes. Do not use undefined "decided the
game" conversions or require rare kills as the sole value metric. Nonlethal
punishment, funding failures, plays and displaced alternatives are also reported.
Diagnostic output stays out of gameplay logs and cannot affect choices.

### Later I5 consumer and ownership

I5 depends on this helper/coverage work. It must define its own conversion,
unknown fallback, response model, domain and gate before raising; this item
alone does not supply them. If evidence supports no conversion, I5 keeps trap
payoff diagnostic and explicitly scopes its initial adoption to supported asset
value rather than silently inventing a trap probability.

| Effect | Owner |
|---|---|
| Mandatory ICE cost, lockout, bypass | I2/security evaluation |
| Private punishment conversion | I5 `trapValue` |
| Shared marginal concealment | L8.8, if separately adopted |
| Role displacement/placement | I3 and the install design |
| Trigger reserve | Existing `AIReserveCredits`/reserve owner |

I5 may change trap play/retention/funding; it must preserve I2's equivalent
active-posture ordinary ICE invariant. Do not demand exact equality of every
observable across changed games: spending and timing will legitimately change.
Measure root-class predictability conditioned on public Grip, credits, turn,
root age and shape, alongside existing tell guards; correlation of any facedown
install with Grip size alone is not proof of a class tell. Reconcile I3's
"tempting server" example through its own gate, not a blanket placement ban.

Combination lines remain a later aim involving I8, declared operation mechanics,
shared click/credit budgets, tag removal/draw opportunities and disruption
fallbacks. No speculative `comboLines` field or combo search ships here.

## Safety and information boundary

Corp-known cards/mechanics and public Runner state only. No policy RNG draws,
posture changes, telemetry from hypothetical probes or hidden Runner identities.
Existing scoring/forced-action/winning-breach safety and reservation overrides
remain authoritative. Observation enables no policy consumer.

## Test scenarios

1. Urtica and Snare diagnostics match declared trigger location, setup, damage,
   tags and costs; prospective HQ diagnostics do not mistake installed-only
   severity zero for no potential punishment.
2. Damage equal to Grip is not lethal; greater damage is conditionally lethal.
   Prevention, larger future Grip, unaffordable triggers and unknown mechanics
   retain explicit assumptions/status instead of promising a win.
3. Setup spending, paid trigger cost and reserved liquidity are distinguished;
   existing reserves are read, never independently spent or double counted.
4. Hidden Runner Grip/Stack substitutions leave all outputs unchanged.
5. Repeated/reordered and throwing probes restore all mutated fields and
   leave RNG, caches, posture epochs and observations unchanged.
6. Scripted access/non-access/early-removal/early-end cohorts have the correct
   denominators; multiple probes produce one real opportunity observation.
7. Central retention and remote installation are separate alternatives/cohorts;
   access, prevented damage and direct flatline events reconcile with outcomes.
8. Start metadata and missing mechanics remain explicit; sparse data never
   becomes a probability of zero or a calibrated estimate.
9. Option off does no diagnostic work; option on changes no choices, reservations,
   gameplay logs or policy RNG state.

Hook results are checked against card gameplay definitions and the engine's
strict damage-greater-than-Grip flatline rule. Run the committed F4 pool with
`trapPlayDiagnostics` off/on, 200 matching paired seeds per deck pair; every
successful pair matches `winner`, `turns`, `logHash` and `decisionHash`, with

No improvement gate applies because no policy changes. Sparse lethal cases

payoff. I5's later policy requires its own strategic gate and supported coverage.
