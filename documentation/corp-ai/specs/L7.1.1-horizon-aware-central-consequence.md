# L7.1.1 Horizon-aware central consequence

**Roadmap item:** L7.1.1 · **Depends on:** F4, L7.1 · **Sets:** none
**Read first:** `documentation/ai-principles.md`, `documentation/corp-ai/principles.md`, the L7.1 ticket's Resolution (`documentation/backlog/feature-layer-7-1-consequence-calibration.md`, or `done/` once reviewed)
**Verified against code:** 143ac4f (2026-10-09)


**Parked 2026-10-09.** The failure analysis below refuted the horizon premise,
so the build described in Design is not justified. I2's spec carries the
lesson (consequence weighting stays inside I2's ranking; HQ and R&D
unweighted). Kept for the evidence.
## Goal
L7.1 weighted the central access penalty by what one breach would expose
*now* (`_breachConsequence(server).weight`) and failed its F4 gate: the Runner
stole more from HQ and R&D, not less (`centralStolen.points` +0.045 per game,
95% interval [+0.001, +0.089], 1,400 paired games). The most likely cause is
the horizon. ICE protects a central for many turns, while a current-snapshot
weight of an agenda-free HQ drops the penalty to a quarter even though agendas
arrive with the next draws. This item tests whether a horizon-aware exposure
(what the central is expected to hold over the protection's lifetime) makes
consequence weighting help, before anything else consumes the weight for
protection.

## Current behaviour
See [architecture: central pressure and breach-loss risk](../architecture.md#central-pressure-and-breach-loss-risk).
`_centralServerThreat()` applies an unweighted access penalty;
`_breachConsequence()` reports the current-snapshot signal that L3.5.1 and I2
plan to consume.

## Design
- First, explain L7.1's failure with evidence: replay a sample of L7.1's
  changed games with `node scripts/ai-batch.js replay ... --diff` (re-adding
  the removed option on a scratch branch) and record which protection choices
  moved and where the extra central steals came from. If the cause is not the
  horizon, rewrite this spec around the cause found before building anything.
- If it is the horizon: add a horizon exposure for HQ and R&D from
  Corp-known information only: the agenda density of the Corp's undrawn cards
  (R&D contents, order-agnostic) applied to the cards one breach accesses,
  blended with the current snapshot. No hidden Runner information. Behind a
  default-off option; `_breachConsequence()` keeps its "if breached now"
  meaning, and the horizon value is a separate field so consumers choose.
- Record what this means for I2's planned `consequenceWeight`, which uses the
  same `0.25 + 0.75 × weight` mapping L7.1 tested.

## Safety and information boundary
- Corp-known contents of its own R&D and HQ only; never R&D order or Runner
  Grip/Stack identities.

## Test scenarios
1. With the option on, an agenda-free HQ in a deck with remaining agendas
   keeps a larger access penalty than an agenda-free HQ in a deck with none
   left.
2. With the option off, penalties are unchanged.
3. Hidden R&D order and Runner Grip/Stack identities change nothing.

## Failure analysis (2026-10-09)

**Verdict:** the horizon is not the cause. L7.1's regression comes from HQ's
protection score being used as a **yardstick** for remote scoring decisions,
not from ICE moving off centrals. Protection ranking alone is neutral.

**Setup.** Scratch branch `scratch/l7-1-failure-analysis` (local, not for
merge) re-adds `consequenceWeightedCentralPressure` at `840a19e` exactly as
the L7.1 plan describes: `accessPenalty = 1.5 × additionalAccess × (0.25 +
0.75 × _breachConsequence(server).weight)` in `_centralServerThreat()`, other
terms unchanged, cap 8. A unit check confirms the formula (agenda-free HQ
penalty 1.5 → 0.375). The re-run reproduces the gate exactly: `node
scripts/ai-batch.js --collector centralStolen [--corp-option
consequenceWeightedCentralPressure=true] --jobs 4`, pool `core-v1`, seeds
1–200, 1,400 paired games, **223 changed**, `centralStolen.points` +0.045
[+0.001, +0.089]. The scratch branch also adds a probe collector
(`l71probe`: every Corp install with HQ/R&D agenda count, access, weight and
ICE count; every steal with turn) and two split modes of the option.

**1. Which channel causes it: split runs (1,400 paired games each).**
`_protectionScore()` feeds two kinds of consumer: protection ranking
(`_rankedServersToProtect()`, where ICE goes) and comparisons against HQ's
score (`_isAScoringServer()`: a remote must score at least HQ;
`_scoringWindow()`: remote minus HQ; also `_bestServerToUpgrade()` and the
consumers near lines 4196 and 6696). The weighted penalty was applied to only
one channel at a time:

| Mode | Changed | `centralStolen.points` | `pointsStolenByServer.rd` | `.remote` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| full option (L7.1) | 223 | +0.045 [+0.001, +0.089] | +0.044 [+0.008, +0.082] | −0.028 [−0.066, +0.010] |
| protection ranking only | 82 | +0.007 [−0.021, +0.036] | +0.006 [−0.018, +0.030] | −0.004 [−0.024, +0.015] |
| everything except ranking ("yardstick") | 156 | +0.048 [+0.011, +0.085] | +0.037 [+0.006, +0.069] | −0.027 [−0.061, +0.006] |

The yardstick channel reproduces the whole regression. Weighting ICE
placement alone neither helps nor hurts. `winRate`, `pointsScored` and
`pointsStolen` are flat in all three runs.

**2. What moved in the 223 changed games.** 66 were worse for central steals
(+185 points), 56 better (−122), and 101 unchanged. Net across changed games:
HQ −6, **R&D +69**, remote −36. The extra central loss is R&D only.
- **First divergence.** Comparing install sequences in the 66 worse games:
  22 begin by **suppressing a remote agenda install**. All 22 have HQ
  multi-access (Docklands Pass, in every pool Runner deck), and in none is HQ
  agenda-free. Another 15 begin by moving ICE off a central (11 to a
  remote or asset, 4 HQ↔R&D swaps); the rest are mixed asset/upgrade/ICE
  reorderings. In 54 of the 65 with an install divergence, HQ had
  multi-access; R&D had it in only 12.
- **Agenda installs swing both ways.** Worse games have 43 fewer remote agenda
  installs (and +20 HQ ICE, +24 remote ICE, +22 assets). Better games have 44
  more. Over all 1,400 games the totals barely change: remote agendas −1,
  ICE HQ +18, R&D +18, remote +62. The option did not strip ICE from
  centrals.
- **Mechanism.** One HQ breach exposes a small share of points needed, so
  HQ's weight is low: median multiplier 0.40, and the weight is 0 in 30% of
  installs where HQ has multi-access. HQ's protection score therefore rises
  by up to 1.1. A remote that used to be "at least as strong as HQ" no
  longer qualifies, and `_scoringWindow()` shrinks. This changes when the Corp
  installs and advances agendas in remotes. In typical cases, HQ held 1–2
  agendas with weight 0.19–0.33 (`pd-tao` 79, 140, 181; `neh-zahya` 29).
- **The extra R&D steals are late and not caused by less R&D ICE.** In 39
  worse games with extra R&D steals, the last one fell after turn 10 in 37.
  At that turn the candidate had the same R&D ICE count as the baseline in
  28, more in 9 and fewer in 2. The steals follow the changed agenda tempo
  (the Runner digs R&D for longer), not a less protected R&D.

**3. The horizon hypothesis, counted.** Only 15 of the 66 worse games start by
taking ICE off a central, and those games account for 17 extra points from
that central (of +185). Just 4 of them took ICE off an **agenda-free HQ**
(`gateway` 142, `pe-steve` 164, `zwicky-magdalene` 93 and 150), costing 3
points, all from `zwicky-magdalene` 150. Nine took ICE off R&D, which always
held agendas but had a low snapshot weight (0.1–0.33), costing 14 points
(for example `gateway` 114: +4; `gateway` 56: +3). Agenda-free HQ is a
minor contributor. Low single-breach weight on R&D is a small one. The
yardstick side effect dominates.

**Cause.** Weighting a server-specific penalty inside `_protectionScore()`
also moves every decision that uses HQ's score as a reference. The weight
is a "one breach now" fraction, typically 0.1–0.3 for HQ under Docklands Pass,
so it raised the HQ bar for remote scoring servers. That reshuffled agenda
timing and moved steals from remotes to R&D. Within ICE placement, the
weighting had no measurable effect.

**Recommendation for I2 (`consequenceWeight = 0.25 + 0.75 × weight`).**
1. Keep it out of `_protectionScore()` and any value another decision
   compares against. Apply it only inside I2's own marginal-security ranking
   of ICE targets. The "ranking only" run shows that scope is safe: neutral,
   with no regression.
2. Do not expect it to help on centrals. The same ranking-only run shows no
   improvement, so the snapshot weight adds no measurable value for HQ/R&D
   ICE choice. Use the weighting for remotes, where it separates empty,
   bluff and agenda servers sharply. For HQ and R&D, use weight 1 (the
   unweighted penalty). Alternatively, gate central weighting as its own
   separate option with its own `--improve`.
3. Raising the floor or adding a horizon term is not supported by this
   evidence as a fix for L7.1. Agenda-free-HQ cases explain 3 of 185 extra
   points. If it is tried, it belongs inside I2's ranking only.

**Implication for this spec.** Following Design step 1 ("if the cause is not
the horizon, rewrite this spec"), the horizon build is not justified as
specified. A rewrite should either retire L7.1.1 or retarget it to the
yardstick coupling: remote scoring eligibility (`_isAScoringServer()`,
`_scoringWindow()`) measured against HQ's protection score, which shifts
whenever any HQ-specific term changes.

**Caveats.**
- First divergence was found from install sequences, so earlier non-install
  decisions (rez, operations, hidden discards) are not resolved. A
  `replay --diff` of all 66 worse games agrees: the first logged hunk is
  mostly a remote root install replaced by credit/draw, or an ICE target
  change. All 66 replay hashes match the report.
- The split is by call path: "ranking" is `_rankedServersToProtect()` only.
  Other ICE paths that read HQ's score count as "yardstick".
- Single pool (`core-v1`); every Runner deck carries Docklands Pass, so HQ
  multi-access is near-constant there.
