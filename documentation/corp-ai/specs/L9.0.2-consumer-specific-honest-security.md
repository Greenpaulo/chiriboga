# L9.0.2 Consumer-specific honest security adoption

**Roadmap item:** L9.0.2 · **Depends on:** L9.0, L9.1, F4 · **Sets:** playable sets (`documentation/card-sets.md`)
**Read first:** `documentation/ai-principles.md`, `documentation/corp-ai/principles.md`, [L9.0.1 experiment](../../backlog/code-review/L9.0.1-adopt-honest-lockout.md), [regression findings](../../corp-ai-regression/findings.md), [I2](I2-ice-selection-by-marginal-security.md), [I4](I4-agenda-installation-and-scoring-commitment.md)

## Goal

Evaluate an initial batch of four honest-verdict consumers: the shared
protection-score bonus, rez reservation, ICE layer admission and critical rez.
Use one independent, default-off option per consumer against a common fresh
control. An apparent winner must pass the unchanged gate again on reserved
fresh seeds before being eligible for the owner's adoption decision.

Keep the other eight consumers as an inventory only: no switches, branches,
fixtures or option-only tests for them in this batch. Present the completed
batch to the owner before adopting an arm or authorising more work. No
combination is justified by adding the effects of isolated arms.

This item remains proposed. On 2026-10-15 the owner rejected L9.0.1's
all-consumer policy and confirmed its failed-gate cleanup. That experiment's
removed global switch is not a prerequisite for these independent local
consumers. No other ticket is reprioritised.

### Separation analysis using existing calibration counts

For each arm, verdict and scope, sum the per-game counters before dividing:
secure breach rate = secureBreached / secure; insecure breach rate =
insecureBreached / insecure. The share of breaches in each group divides that
group's breached count by secureBreached + insecureBreached. A breached count
means a snapshotted server had at least one successful run that Runner turn;
it is not a count of all runs, steals or points. Only the collector's servers
with stakes are observed. This is descriptive analysis, not a new adoption gate.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|

| All stakes, honest | 3,813 / 136 | 3.57% | 79,236 / 12,292 | 15.51% | 1.09% | 98.91% |

|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All stakes, current | 28,109 / 1,744 | 6.20% | 58,127 / 11,218 | 19.30% | 13.45% | 86.55% |

| Agenda remotes, honest | 554 / 25 | 4.51% | 5,575 / 1,191 | 21.36% | 2.06% | 97.94% |


In the baseline, secure coverage is 33.48% for the current verdict versus
4.59% for honest (agenda remotes: 48.65% versus 7.70%). The honest insecure
group is correspondingly much broader. Its capture of almost all breaches
therefore cannot establish superior discrimination: its insecure group also
has a lower breach rate than the current insecure group. The groups differ,
and aggregate counts do not supply a joint reclassification table or matched
per-server predictions. Do not infer calibration, causality or a statistically
better separator from the secure-group percentages alone.

Hedged conclusion: the honest verdict carries a real signal, consistent with
its low observed secure-group breach rates, while switching what secure means
for every consumer at once coincided with worse results. The agenda-holding
mechanism is inferred, not demonstrated. All observations concern the stock
Runner AI, which runs constantly; a human may breach more or less often.

## Design

1. **Local selection only.** Add only the first four inventory options,
   default false. Do not implement rows 5–12.
   Each consumer reads honestIsSecure (and honestLockout / honest cost fields
   only where relevant) in its own branch. Never overwrite the public cached
   compatibility result, its reasons, ranking entry.isSecure or entry.security.
   Leave the evaluator, `_icePlanIsBetter()` and other consumers unchanged.
2. **Prevent leakage through supplied results.** `_protectionScore()` and
   `_serverRunPressure()` accept a supplied security record. The score arm
   selects its local boolean from the raw two-verdict record without mutating
   that record or changing what run pressure reads. Keep ranked entries raw;
   allocation, debt and emergency recovery retain their compatibility verdicts.
   Do not add options for these deferred consumers or a global selector on
   materialization. The +2 score arm legitimately affects all callers of that
   shared score, including relative scoring eligibility; this indirect effect
   is part of the arm. Do not describe it as allocation-only.
3. **Explainability and isolation.** Record consumer name, option, old and
   selected verdict, selected reasons and unknown-price state in the relevant
   decision trace. Diagnostics must agree with the actual local policy and
   preserve unrelated trace meanings. A logging-only change is not sufficient
   proof of policy reachability: require a deterministic changed decision for
   each live consumer in addition to the harness changed-game guard.
4. **Null means unknown (L9.1).** Test `value === null` before arithmetic,
   comparisons or formatting of honestMandatoryBreakCost and honestBreakCost.
   Never turn null into zero, Infinity, affordability, a secure verdict or a
   cost delta. An independently proven lockout may still establish security;
   null itself must neither establish nor erase one. Known lower bounds remain
   explicitly labelled lower bounds;
   they are not exact prices. Boolean-only consumers must use the computed
   honest verdict instead of reconstructing it from costs. Test unknown-price
   records for every option, including supplied records, before/after probes
   and unchanged opposite consumers. No new cost heuristic or coefficient.
5. **Frozen independent arms.** Stage only the four initial switches behind
   defaults off and verify control identity before freezing a source revision.
   Within each seed cohort, compare every candidate alone against the same
   effective control, source revision and deck pool. Keep all four switches
   off in every control and the other three off in each candidate. Complete
   the batch's discovery assessments before selecting apparent winners; do not
   stop at the first pass. Keep source and policy fixed through confirmation,
   including losing switches temporarily off until measurement is complete.
   Do not enable an early winner while evaluating another arm. Source or
   prerequisite-policy changes invalidate affected comparisons and require
   fresh controls/repeated assessments. Do not reuse L9.0.1's historical
   reports as the new control. Remove rejected switches after measurement and
   the owner's disposition; this ticket does not retain them indefinitely.
6. **Owner decisions.** Report every initial arm's discovery result and every
   required confirmation result before changing defaults, ticket status or
   removing a candidate. Stop at an owner checkpoint after this batch whether
   zero, one or several arms appear promising. A changed game is reachability,
   not benefit; no early winner is required to justify discussing other
   consumers. Do not implement the remaining eight without authorisation.
   No combined option is authorized here. Any combination requires its own preregistered test against an
   appropriate fresh control; conditional effects from regression findings
   must not be added together.

### Testing order and saved-report limits

Test only the first four inventory rows, in order: shared scoring/protection
bonus, ordinary rez reservation, ICE layer admission, critical rez. This
prioritises scoring and ICE-credit commitments because scoring fell while
theft moved to centrals. The saved reports do not prove which consumer caused
that pattern. This is an experimental order for this batch, not a reordering
of other roadmap tickets. Review the completed batch with the owner before
any further implementation; the other eight remain inventory only.

The reports contain game-level hashes/outcomes/metrics, not per-decision
consumer names, available actions, selected actions or reasons. All 1,219
log-changed pairs also have changed decision hashes; 1,221 decision hashes
changed in total. These identify changed decision streams, not which function
caused the first divergence. Thus the reports cannot attribute the 1,219
changed pairs to scoring, rez, draw or allocation. Do not claim that they do,
and do not generate new games to fill that gap as part of this documentation
work. Future implementation can capture/replay first divergences, but their
scope and findings must be reported separately.

**Dormant strict floor (inventory only).** With secureScoringServerGate=false,
honestScoringGate could not change a choice at line 712. Do not implement
that switch or its conditional tests in this batch, and do not turn the old
strict gate on to manufacture coverage. Any future conditional control would
need a separate owner-approved design. Indirect scoring changes through the
initial shared-score arm remain live with the strict gate off.

### Batch checkpoint and outcome interpretation

- Discovery without a clear improvement is an inconclusive/no-demonstrated-gain
  result, not adoption evidence. Zero policy effect is a reachability result.
- A discovery pass without a confirmation pass is not eligible for adoption.
  Report the confirmation as failed or inconclusive according to its evidence;
  do not claim that lack of significance proves no effect.
- Present all four discovery results, including nulls and guards, plus every
  confirmation result to the owner before changing defaults or proceeding.
- Keep the pointsStolen improvement requirement and all registered guards for
  this first batch. Later draw/purge or other consumers may need a primary
  outcome that matches their purpose; any such choice or revised gate needs
  owner approval and preregistration before those candidate results are seen.
  Do not import an opportunistic any-metric-wins rule from another item.

guard; the winRate, scoring and length guards are retained unchanged. Present
