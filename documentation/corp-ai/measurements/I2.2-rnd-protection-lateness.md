# I2.2 R&D protection investigation

Investigated at `d7f2713`, 2026-10-19. No policy, default, card hook or
calibration constant changed. [Ticket](../../backlog/I2.2-rnd-protection-lateness-investigation.md).

## Disposition

Retain adopted I2 allocation. Its measured R&D loss is a real trade-off, with
a reproducible opening-ranking mechanism, rather than a demonstrated rules
defect. The adopted 7,000-pair gate reduced total theft and increased scoring
and wins. A blanket earlier-R&D rule is not supported: delayed R&D protection
also occurs in winning counterexamples with lower R&D theft. No repair policy
or new option is adopted or proposed from these observations alone.

This closes the investigation's allocation question before I3 design freeze;
it does not certify that I2's ranking or hazard estimates are optimal. The
fraction of aggregate additional theft mediated by opening timing, rather
than later divergent play, is unidentified. Selecting a few losing replays
cannot estimate that fraction.

## Mechanism and competing explanations

In btl-kit/1610, turn 1 starts with 5 credits, 3 clicks and one Hortum
(printed rez 4). R&D, HQ and the new remote are admitted with
`noUnrezzedIce`; all three offer Hortum in the affordable group. All have
zero install cost and planned rez cost 4. The returned measurements are:

| Destination | Stake | Hazard before → after | Expected points saved | Credit score |
| --- | ---: | --- | ---: | ---: |
| R&D | 0.394737 | 0.2085 → 0.1913 | 0.013579 | −3.940253 |
| HQ | 0.5 | 0.1539 → 0.1066 | 0.0473 | −3.791880 |
| New scoring remote | 3 | 0.2415 → 0.2015 | 0.186840 | −3.177904 |

I2 chooses the remote by score, while the legacy arm selects R&D. On the
next ranking Hortum is gone from HQ; R&D remains admitted but has no ICE
candidate. The on arm installs and advances Send a Message in that remote.
This is direct evidence for **ranking then inventory depletion**, not an
opening R&D admission failure or inability to pay for Hortum. The same
ranking mechanism with Ice Wall in seed 1603 produces a favourable outcome.
The remote's two-turn exposure is intentional here: clicks remain to install
its agenda, so the existing one-turn delay does not apply.

R&D's smaller marginal return in this example combines a lower per-access
stake with a smaller calibrated hazard drop. Neither quantity alone is a
proven defect. The table estimates actual stock-Runner breach frequency,
not rules-based certainty of blocking a run. Replacing it with a guarantee,
or equalising server stakes, would be a strategic experiment.

**Admission:** an already protected server can later be rejected as
`existingUnrezzedIceAndPoor`; the probe records that reason separately.
Such rejections affect layers, but do not explain seed 1610's bare R&D.

**Affordability/suitability:** seed 1622 has two printed-cost-3 Magnets and
5 credits, yet the existing card-owned `AIWorthwhileIce` rejects their
install suitability when no ice-hosted Runner programs exist. Both are
therefore in the low-priority list, even at a hypothetical 7 credits. A
cash-only explanation would be wrong. Once low priorities are considered,
the remote still outranks R&D (−2.177904 versus −2.948219). Only one Magnet
is installed, on that remote, before the Runner wins on turn 4. Do not count
the card hook's suitability verdict as a missing legal engine option.

**Costs/ties:** pd-tao/1601 gives Eli 1.0 a score of −3 at R&D, HQ and the
empty designated remote: all measured hazard drops are zero, so the retained
walk order chooses R&D. In neh-zahya/1601, Ping on R&D scores −1.9296 versus
−2 on a zero-stake remote and −2 on HQ; the cheaper ICE wins and R&D is
protected on turn 1. I2 does not categorically prefer remotes or omit R&D.

**Measurement:** `iceInstallsByServer.firstTurn` uses zero for no install;
zero is not “turn zero”. Conditional means must be accompanied by counts of
never-protected games. Completed installation is also not equivalent to a
rezzed barrier or a prevented breach. Later ICE affordability and Runner
choices can diverge after the opening, so timing/theft correlation alone
cannot quantify mediation or identify a universally better allocation.

## Constraints carried forward

**I3:** preserve early-R&D, delayed-R&D, no-R&D and beneficial HQ/remote cases
in its evidence. Replace the designated remote's stake/exposure rule with
the proposed persistent role deliberately; do not simply add more future
remote points without accounting for the agendas' competing HQ/R&D exposure
and installation timing. Report R&D and HQ theft separately and accompany
conditional first-ICE timing with never-protected and unprotected-through-turn-2
rates. Gate each policy against a fresh matched control; do not inherit I2's
historical success or introduce a mandatory central-first rule from this sample.

**I9:** recheck M2 reliability under the adopted allocation and later I3
roles, separating server kind, public ICE-count group, early turn, deck pair
and margin bucket, with cell support and held-out uncertainty. The opening
cases expose coarse/zero marginal differences and differing hazard drops;
they do not establish that any particular cell or the 4.4 point value is
miscalibrated. Recalibration and any consuming policy change need their own
frozen evidence and gates. Audit the residual R&D trade-off before retiring
the legacy path, alongside the existing empty-Archives disposition.
